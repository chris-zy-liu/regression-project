\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{5pt}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Data and evaluation}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Reconstructed Census data; nested temporal validation; paired block-bootstrap comparisons.
  \item \textbf{Result:} 706 forecasts at 10 dates (635 historical + 71 already in the original prospective check). No additional Census release beyond that check; original baseline reproduced.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Industry-specific models}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Compared separate industry regressions and partial pooling with pooled OLS.
  \item \textbf{Result:} Industry-model MAE: \textbf{3.04 percentage points}, versus 3.30 for pooled OLS and 3.39 for the recent average; 10.3\% improvement over the average.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nonlinear subset selection}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Searched 320 hierarchical predictor-block combinations, including squares and interactions.
  \item \textbf{Result:} Expected use squared selected at 6/10 dates; current-by-expected use at 4/10. Current use squared and time squared never selected. MAE: \textbf{3.32}; no improvement over baseline.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Advanced models and added predictors}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Tested ridge, elastic net, and gradient boosting with lags, rolling averages, aggregate rates, interactions, and panel-cycle terms.
  \item \textbf{Result:} MAE: elastic net \textbf{3.07}, boosting \textbf{3.12}, ridge \textbf{3.13}, full OLS \textbf{5.68}. Removing history worsened ridge to 3.70; cycle terms added little.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Industry growth and ranking uncertainty}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Estimated industry trends with group intercepts; used block-bootstrap intervals and ranks.
  \item \textbf{Result:} Leading growth: finance 1.28, Information 1.22, professional services 1.08 percentage points per four weeks. Finance and Information cannot be confidently ordered.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Overparameterization}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Varied random-feature counts above and below training-row counts; compared minimum-norm least squares with ridge.
  \item \textbf{Result:} Minimum-norm fits interpolate training data but produce extreme future errors. Ridge stabilizes the larger representations; unregularized overparameterization is not competitive.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Benchmark scores and nonstationary LLM releases}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Method:} Added ECI/GPQA scores, Poisson regression for a time-varying release rate, and regression for future capability marks; tested arrival predictors and industry interactions.
  \item \textbf{Result:} Forecast: \textbf{12.48 catalog releases in 42 days} (conditional 90\% range: 6--20). Benchmark/NHPP boosting MAE: 3.120 versus 3.115 for its control; no clear improvement. Release batching weakens Poisson assumptions.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Remaining questions}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item Do gains persist on future releases? Can official workbooks verify the extract and add predictors?
  \item Are rankings and nonlinear expectation terms stable? Does interpolation behavior survive other feature choices?
  \item Can benchmark publication histories and release-batch models improve the Poisson analysis? What evidence could identify adoption effects?
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
